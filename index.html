Hearing screening at your workplace

On-site hearing tests for your staff, with minimal disruption to your day and the report you need for your records.

Why it matters

Under Maltese law (S.L. 646.20), employers must protect staff from noise at work, including hearing checks for those exposed above set noise levels. Routine baseline and annual audiograms help you stay compliant and give you clear, documented evidence that helps defend against hearing loss claims.

What is included

  • Baseline hearing tests for new and existing staff
  • Annual retests to track any change over time
  • On-site screening with calibrated, up-to-date digital equipment, so staff don't need to leave work
  • Individual results explained to each employee
  • A clear report for your health and safety records
  • Staff talks on protecting hearing at work
  • Custom ear protection for your team

How it works

  1. Get in touch. Share how many staff you have and what kind of work they do.
  2. On-site visit. Screening on your premises, scheduled around your shifts.
  3. Results on the day. Each employee has their result explained before they leave.
  4. Your report. A clear summary for your records, with any follow-ups flagged.
  5. Every year. Annual retests compare against the baseline.

Hearing aids that sound natural

The hearing aids are from Bernafon, a Swiss brand known for natural sound and for helping with high-frequency hearing loss, the most common kind.

High-frequency loss often shows up as trouble with women's and children's voices, or with sounds like "s", "f" and "th". Conversations seem mumbled, especially in busy places. The right hearing aid brings those sounds back without making everything louder.

Styles to suit you

  • Behind the ear Small and discreet, sitting neatly behind your ear.
  • In the ear Custom-made to your ear, some nearly invisible.
  • Extra powerful For more severe hearing loss.

Common questions

Does a hearing test hurt?

No. The test is painless. You listen to soft sounds through headphones and respond when you hear them. Tympanometry, if needed, is a gentle puff of air pressure in the ear that takes a few seconds.

How long does the appointment take?

Between 30 and 45 minutes, including time to go through your results and options.

Can I bring someone with me?

Yes. Many people bring a family member or friend, and it often helps to have a familiar voice in the room when discussing hearing aids.

What should I bring?

A list of your current medication and any relevant medical history. If you already wear hearing aids, bring them along too.
